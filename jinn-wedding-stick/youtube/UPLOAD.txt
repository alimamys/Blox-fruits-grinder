When I was a little girl, my grandmother told me a story in the majlis. I have never forgotten it.
Sabahiya, Kuwait, 1997. Noura, the most famous taqaqa (wedding singer) in Kuwait, gets a call from "the mother of
the bride": a wedding tomorrow night, five hundred dinars. A silent driver, a house nobody had ever seen, a hall of
gold - and guests who clapped like one person, whose eyes shone in the dark, and whose feet were not feet.

If a stranger offered you far too much to sing at a wedding you'd never heard of... would you ask whose wedding it is?

About the story: "The Jinn Wedding" (عرس الجن) is a Kuwaiti urban legend that has been told for decades, in many
versions. Nobody has ever proved it happened, and nobody has ever proved it didn't. We tell it as a legend: the names,
the place and the year are the legend's own, and the wording, the small details and the grandmother's frame story are
ours. It is not a claim about any real person. The newspaper page in the film is our own recreation in the style
of a 1990s Kuwaiti daily; the paper's name is invented.
