# The Jinn Wedding (عرس الجن)

A retelling of the Kuwaiti urban legend of **Noura the taqaqa** (الطقاقة نورة), the wedding singer who, people say,
was hired to play a wedding in **Sabahiya** in **1997** and found that none of the guests were human. Told as a
legend: nobody has ever proved it happened, and nobody has ever proved it didn't.

Frame: a little girl sits with her grandmother - her **yadda** (Kuwaiti for grandmother) - in the majlis on a winter
evening (floor cushions, the coffee dallah, incense smoking in the mabkhara). Yadda tells her the story; the picture
zooms from the majlis into Noura's night in 1997 and comes back to the majlis at the most frightening moments.

The names, place, year and the main events are the legend's own (Noura the taqaqa and her band of women, the call
from "the mother of the bride", the 500 dinars, the night drive, the lavish house, guests whose feet were not human,
terrible sounds, the band too afraid to stop playing, the man at dawn who asks why they are at an abandoned house,
Noura never singing at a wedding again, the story printed without her name). The wording and the
small details are ours.

Target length: under 7 minutes. English, with Gulf voices.

## 1. The majlis

NARRATOR (the girl, grown up):
Once, a long time ago, when I was a little girl, my grandmother told me a story. I have never forgotten it.

(The majlis. Incense. Yadda pours coffee from the dallah.)
GIRL (7): Yadda, tell me a scary one. A true one.
YADDA: A true one? ... Nobody knows if this one is true. But everybody in Kuwait knows it.
YADDA: It happened in Sabahiya, in nineteen ninety-seven. To a singer called Noura.

(Title: THE JINN WEDDING - عرس الجن. The picture zooms into the incense smoke, into 1997.)

## 2. The call

YADDA:
In those days, Noura was the most famous taqaqa in Kuwait. A wedding singer. She and her band of women, with their
drums and their old songs, were booked months ahead, all over the Gulf.
One quiet evening, the phone rang. A woman's voice. She said she was the mother of the bride.

MOTHER OF THE BRIDE (on the phone, warm, too calm): We are having a wedding tomorrow night, in Sabahiya. My
daughter wants only you.
NOURA: I'm sorry. We're fully booked.
MOTHER OF THE BRIDE: Five hundred dinars.

YADDA:
Five hundred dinars, in those days, was a fortune. So Noura said yes.

## 3. The drive

YADDA:
The next night, a car came for them. The driver never said one word.
They drove past the last houses of Sabahiya, and kept going, into the dark.
Then, out of nowhere, a house. Huge, and blazing with light.
Noura had sung in every big house in Kuwait. She had never seen this one.

## 4. The hall

YADDA:
Inside, the women's hall was gold from the floor to the ceiling. Hundreds of guests in shining dresses. So much
incense the air was blue. And at the far end, on a stage, the bride, under a heavy veil.
The mother of the bride took Noura's hand. Her fingers were ice cold.

MOTHER OF THE BRIDE: Play. And do not stop until the bride has gone.

YADDA:
So they played. And the guests danced.
Hundreds of hands, clapping on exactly the same beat. Like one person.
Nobody touched the food. Nobody spoke. And when the lights flickered, their eyes shone, like a cat's eyes in the
dark.

## 5. The feet

YADDA:
The youngest girl in the band leaned close to Noura, still drumming.

DRUMMER (whispering, terrified): Noura. Look at their feet.

YADDA:
Under the long dresses, where their feet should be, there were hooves. Some like a goat. Some like a donkey.
Clicking on the marble, in time with the drums.

(The majlis. The girl pulls a cushion over her face.)
GIRL: Yadda! Did they run away?
YADDA: Run? Where to? Nobody knew where that house was.

DRUMMER: We have to go.
NOURA (smiling, through her teeth): Keep playing. Whatever you see, do not stop.

YADDA:
And under the music, they began to hear other sounds. Something braying behind the walls. Laughing that was not
laughing. And voices singing along with Noura's songs, in a language none of them knew.

## 6. Until dawn

YADDA:
They played all night. Their hands cracked and bled on the drums. They smiled, and sang, and never looked down.
The bride never lifted her veil.
Just before dawn, the mother of the bride raised her hand, and the hall went silent.

MOTHER OF THE BRIDE: Enough. Thank you, Noura. You were everything they said.

YADDA:
They were paid, exactly as promised. And they walked out into the grey morning, shaking, without a word.

## 7. The man at dawn

YADDA:
Outside, a man was standing in the sand, staring at them.

MAN: What are you doing here? Nobody lives in this house. It has been empty for years.

YADDA:
Slowly, Noura turned around.
The house was a ruin. Broken windows. Sand blowing through the doorway. The walls black from an old fire.
No lights. No cars. No music.
Only their footprints in the sand. And all around them, hundreds of small hoofprints.

## 8. The majlis again

YADDA:
After that night, Noura never sang at a wedding again. People say she never told anyone why.
A newspaper printed the story, without her name. And since then, it has been told a thousand ways.

(The front page of a 1990s Kuwaiti daily, in Arabic, with an English translation strip. The paper's name,
"Sawt Al-Deera", is invented.)

GIRL: Is it true, Yadda?
YADDA: Nobody ever proved it happened. And nobody ever proved it didn't.
YADDA: But listen to me, habibti. If a stranger ever calls you, and offers far too much to sing at a wedding you
never heard of...
YADDA: ...ask whose wedding it is.

(The old phone in the majlis rings. They both look at it. Black.)
