# Source script (verbatim from the brief)

Full 10-minute horror screenplay

## Scene 1 — The frame that shouldn't exist
00:00–00:45 · Cold open · Quiet, uneasy voice

Visual: Completely black screen. A faint computer fan hums. A mouse clicks. We see a digital animation timeline, followed by a rapid flash of a tall human figure in an empty hallway.

MARA (narration):

There were 239 frames in my animation when I went to bed.

When I opened it again, there were 240.

In the extra frame, a man was standing in my hallway.

I hadn't drawn him.

And the next time I pressed play, he was closer.

I know how ridiculous that sounds.

A drawing can't move by itself. An animation can't create new frames.

At least, that's what I believed.

Until that night.

Visual: The figure suddenly appears at the end of the hallway. Its head is bent unnaturally to one side. Cut to black.

Audio: A single, dry scratching sound. No musical sting.

## Scene 2 — A normal night
00:45–01:40 · Establishing the protagonist

Visual: A modest apartment at night. Rain on a window. A computer monitor illuminates a woman in her early thirties wearing an oversized burnt-orange sweatshirt.

MARA (narration):

My name is Mara. I'm a freelance animator.

Most nights, I work until two or three in the morning. It's quieter then. No phone calls. No interruptions.

That night, I was finishing a short animation of an empty apartment hallway.

Nothing complicated. A flickering ceiling light, a few shadows, and a slow movement toward a closed bedroom door.

I'd used my own hallway as a reference.

The little crack above the doorframe, the old carpet, even the stain on the ceiling. It was all there.

I'd been staring at the same ten seconds of animation for nearly three hours.

Eventually, I saved the project, closed my eyes for a moment, and checked the timeline one last time.

That's when I noticed the extra frame.

Visual: Camera pushes slowly toward the computer screen. The cursor stops on frame 240.

## Scene 3 — The stranger
01:40–02:35 · First supernatural reveal

Visual: Extreme close-up of the animation timeline. Mara advances frame by frame.

MARA:

At first, I thought I'd accidentally duplicated something.

I clicked on the final frame.

There was a man standing at the far end of the hallway.

He was very tall. Taller than the doorframe.

His arms hung straight down, and his head tilted slightly forward, as though he were examining something on the floor.

His face was almost completely hidden.

But something about the drawing bothered me.

It matched my animation style perfectly.

The same brush texture. The same lighting. Even the same shadows.

I checked the layers.

Background. Lighting. Ceiling. Door.

There was no layer containing a man.

I tried selecting him.

Nothing.

He was part of the picture.

Then I pressed play again.

Visual: Eight-second animation plays. At the last instant, the figure is now standing halfway along the hallway.

MARA (whispering):

He had moved.

## Scene 4 — One step closer
02:35–03:30 · Escalation

Visual: Alternating close-ups of Mara's eyes, the computer screen, and the real dark hallway behind her.

MARA:

I stared at the screen for a long time.

Then I played the animation again.

The man was closer.

Not much. Maybe a meter.

I played it a third time.

Closer.

A fourth time.

Closer.

He didn't walk.

There was no movement between positions.

He simply appeared somewhere new every time the animation reached its final frame.

Like someone was drawing him in a different place.

I stopped the playback.

For several seconds, I didn't move.

Then I heard something from my actual hallway.

Audio: A faint, slow scratch against a hard surface.

It sounded like someone dragging a pencil across a wall.

I turned around.

The hallway was empty.

But the ceiling light was flickering.

Exactly like the one in my animation.

## Scene 5 — The warning
03:30–04:25 · Second voice enters

Visual: Mara calls her friend Hana. Phone screen glows against the dark room.

MARA:

I called Hana.

She'd worked with me on animation projects before. If anyone could explain this, she could.

HANA (sleepy):

Mara? It's almost three in the morning.

MARA:

I need you to look at something. I'm sending you a screenshot.

Pause. Phone notification.

HANA:

What's this?

MARA:

Tell me if you can see the man in the hallway.

HANA:

Yeah. He's creepy. Did you draw him?

MARA:

No.

HANA:

What do you mean, no?

MARA:

He wasn't there before. And every time I play the animation, he gets closer.

Silence.

HANA (now serious):

Okay. Stop playing it. Close the program.

MARA:

I already tried. It keeps reopening the same frame.

HANA:

Then turn the computer off. And get out of that apartment.

Audio: A scratching sound comes from the hallway again, louder this time.

## Scene 6 — The hallway changes
04:25–05:25 · First major scare

Visual: Mara picks up her phone and cautiously approaches the hallway. A weak flashlight beam moves over the walls.

MARA:

I grabbed my keys.

I wasn't going to spend another second trying to understand it.

But when I reached the hallway, something was wrong.

The carpet looked different.

The same stain appeared every few feet, repeating in a perfect pattern.

The crack above the bedroom door was no longer just a crack.

It was a thin, black line. Almost like a pencil mark.

I moved closer.

The entire wall looked wrong.

Too smooth. Too flat.

Like someone had painted over a photograph.

I reached out and touched the surface.

It felt like paper.

Visual: Mara presses one finger against the wall. A faint black line follows her fingertip.

Then, from behind the bedroom door, I heard a sound.

Three gentle taps.

Audio: Tap. Tap. Tap.

The same scratching noise I'd heard through my computer speakers.

I ran back to the desk.

And for the first time, I understood.

The animation wasn't copying my hallway anymore.

My hallway was copying the animation.

## Scene 7 — Trying to erase him
05:25–06:25 · False solution

Visual: Mara frantically opens the drawing tools on her computer.

MARA:

I needed to know whether I could change it.

I picked up my stylus and drew a red line across the hallway wall in the animation.

Immediately, I heard a long scraping noise.

I looked behind me.

A red line had appeared on my real wall.

The exact same length.

The exact same position.

My hands started shaking.

If the animation could change the apartment, maybe I could use it to stop the man.

I selected a thick white brush.

Then I started painting over him.

First his legs.

Then his arms.

Then the strange angle of his head.

I kept painting until the entire figure disappeared into the white background.

I flattened the layer.

Saved the project.

And waited.

Audio: The scratching suddenly stops. Absolute silence.

For a few seconds, nothing happened.

I thought I'd done it.

I thought I'd erased him.

But I hadn't erased him.

I'd made a much worse mistake.

## Scene 8 — White on white
06:25–07:30 · The central twist

Visual: The hallway's colors gradually fade. Shadows disappear. The wall becomes a flat, almost featureless white.

MARA:

I noticed the changes slowly.

The wallpaper had lost its texture.

The shadows around the doorframe had disappeared.

Even the stains were gone.

My apartment looked like an unfinished drawing.

I called Hana again.

MARA:

I think I got rid of him.

HANA:

What did you do?

MARA:

I painted over him. He's gone.

There was a long silence.

HANA:

Mara, listen to me.

MARA:

What?

HANA:

I'm looking at the screenshot you sent earlier.

MARA:

Okay?

HANA:

I zoomed in on the man.

Visual: Phone screen. The silhouetted man's shape is barely visible.

HANA:

The light is casting a shadow behind him.

MARA:

So?

HANA:

Look at where the shadow ends.

Visual: A pale shape appears on the wall behind the figure.

HANA (quietly):

He's standing right against the white wall.

Mara turned toward her own wall.

For the first time, she noticed a faint outline.

Not black.

Not gray.

White.

Almost the exact same color as the paint.

Visual: A barely visible shoulder-shaped outline slowly separates from the wall.

MARA (breathless):

Oh God.

Audio: A slow, wet peeling sound.

## Scene 9 — Inside the wall
07:30–08:35 · Escalating physical horror

Visual: Camera tracks slowly along the white wall. A subtle raised shape suggests a human body beneath the surface.

MARA:

I backed away.

The shape moved.

Not like a person walking.

More like a drawing being stretched across paper.

The outline of a hand appeared near the ceiling.

Then an elbow.

Then the curve of a head.

I could barely see it unless I tilted my phone flashlight sideways.

And the worst part was how quietly it moved.

No breathing.

No footsteps.

Just that horrible scratching sound.

It was coming from inside the walls now.

I grabbed the computer and tried to smash the screen.

But the image stayed there.

The man was gone from the drawing.

In his place was a blank white space.

And through that blank space, something was slowly pushing outward.

Visual: White fingers emerge just far enough to cast tiny shadows.

I heard Hana shouting through my phone.

HANA (panicking):

Mara! Get out! Get out right now!

I ran for the front door.

There was no handle.

There wasn't even a door anymore.

Just a smooth white rectangle where the entrance had been.

Audio: A low impact from inside the wall.

## Scene 10 — The recording
08:35–09:25 · Nearly silent, intimate terror

Visual: Inside a dark storage closet. Mara sits with her knees pulled against her chest. Only her phone illuminates her face.

MARA (narration, now almost whispering):

That's where I am now.

Inside the storage closet.

I started recording this because I don't know what else to do.

I can still hear it moving around outside.

Sometimes the scratching stops.

And when it does, I hear something even worse.

A soft dragging sound.

Like someone tracing a finger over paper.

I've been watching the gap beneath the closet door.

There hasn't been a shadow.

Not once.

I think I finally understand why.

When I painted over that man, I didn't destroy him.

I changed his color.

I made him invisible against anything white.

And nearly every wall in this apartment is white.

Audio: Scratching stops. Silence for four seconds.

There's something touching the closet door.

## Scene 11 — The last selection
09:25–10:00 · Final reveal and abrupt ending

Visual: Close-up of the white closet door from Mara's perspective. A tiny indentation develops near the handle.

MARA (shaking):

There's a face.

I can see it now.

It's pressed against the other side.

But it's not coming through.

It's just... looking at me.

The white surface begins to bulge inward.

Wait.

There are little lines appearing around me.

On the floor.

On the walls.

They're moving.

Like...

Like a selection box.

Visual: A rectangle of tiny animated dashes appears around Mara, expanding across the closet.

Oh no.

Someone's selecting me.

Sound: A distant computer mouse click.

MARA (barely audible):

I'm not the one drawing anymore.

Visual: A huge white cursor appears against the dark closet wall. It stops directly above Mara.

Sound: One final click.

Cut to pure white.

Hold complete silence for three seconds.

END.

## Production notes (from the brief)

- **Character and lighting:** natural human proportions, expressive but subtle facial animation, limited movement, realistic shadows, and cold moonlight contrasted with warm screen illumination.
- **Environments and movement:** reuse the apartment, hallway and closet as layered compositions. Animate slow camera pushes, flickering lights, creeping silhouettes and slight parallax, rather than constant full-body motion.
- **Creature design:** keep the entity mostly hidden. The white-on-white reveal should require close attention. Its movement should look subtly unnatural, like pieces of a drawing being repositioned.

| production setting | recommendation |
| --- | --- |
| Video format | 1920 × 1080, 16:9 |
| Export frame rate | 24 fps |
| Animation technique | layered 2D illustrations, parallax, limited character animation |
| Characters | Mara and Hana (voice only for Hana) |
| Narrator | female, early 30s, conversational and increasingly frightened |
| Sound | pencil scratches, subtle electronic hum, faint rain, silence, occasional low-frequency impacts |
| Duration | target 10 minutes, adjusted to the actual voice performance |
| Editing | slow suspense shots alternating with brief, disturbing reveals |

Use a restrained female narration that gradually shifts from curiosity to controlled panic. Avoid a constant horror soundtrack: the absence of sound makes the scratching, the mouse clicks and the final reveal considerably more effective.
