# The Rake — "It Visits Three Times"

An original story built on "The Rake", an anonymous internet legend (4chan, 2005): a pale, hairless, starved
figure with long claws that crouches at the foot of a bed and watches people sleep. The legend has no single
author; the characters, events, rule and ending below are our own.

Target length: about 6 to 7 minutes.

## 1. The first night

NARRATOR (Lily at 34, quiet, close):
When I was nine, I woke up at four in the morning, and something was sitting on my bed.
Not at the end of it, where a dog would sleep. It was halfway up the blanket, crouched over my knees.
It was shaped like a man. A very thin man. No hair. No clothes. Skin the colour of candle wax.
Its hands were spread on the blanket, and the fingers were far too long. Each one ended in a curved yellow claw, like the teeth of a rake.
And it was looking at me.
It didn't blink. It didn't breathe. It just watched.
I pulled the covers over my face and counted to ten.
When I looked again, it was gone.

TITLE: THE RAKE

## 2. Grandma's house

NARRATOR:
That summer, we had moved into my grandmother's old house. She'd died in the spring, and my dad couldn't bear to sell it.
I got the little bedroom at the end of the hall. The room that used to be his.
At breakfast, I told him.

LILY (9): Dad, there was a man on my bed last night. A skinny man with really long fingers.
DAD: That's a nightmare, bug. New house, new noises. Everybody gets them.
LILY: It was four o'clock. I looked at the clock.

NARRATOR:
My dad stopped buttering his toast.
Just for a second. Then he smiled, kissed the top of my head, and told me to stop reading scary books before bed.
I didn't read scary books.

## 3. The diary

NARRATOR:
That weekend, Dad sent me up to the attic to find the box fan.
Behind the boxes there was a little blue notebook with a broken lock. My grandmother's handwriting. Nineteen eighty-four.
Most of it was boring. Church. Groceries. The weather. And then, near the end, the handwriting changed.

GRANDMA (the diary, read in her voice):
June eleventh. Something came into the house last night.
It sat on Danny's bed and watched him sleep.
My mother told me about it when I was a girl. I thought it was a story to keep children in bed.
The first time, it only watches. The second time, it comes closer. The third time, it says your name.
Danny sleeps in our room now. I will not let it come back.

NARRATOR:
That was the last page. The rest had been torn out.
My dad's name is Daniel. When he was a boy, everyone called him Danny.
And his room, the little bedroom at the end of the hall, was mine now.

## 4. Dad reads it

NARRATOR:
I showed him the diary. I watched him read it twice.

DAD: Where did you find this?
LILY: Dad, it said the second time it comes closer. It was on my bed. It was right on top of me.
DAD: I know, bug. I know.

NARRATOR:
He didn't say "it was a dream" this time.
He said I was sleeping with Mom tonight. And he would sleep in my room.

LILY: But what if it comes back?
DAD: Then it'll find me.

## 5. The third night

NARRATOR:
I tried to stay awake. I couldn't.
I woke up at four in the morning.
The house was completely silent. No clock. No wind. Nothing.
And then, through the wall, very softly, I heard a voice I had never heard before. Thin and dry, like paper tearing.

THE RAKE (a dry whisper, through the wall): Danny.

NARRATOR:
Mom didn't wake up. I still don't know how she didn't wake up.
I ran down the hall. My bedroom door was open. The lamp was on.
And the bed was empty.
The blanket had been torn open in three long lines, from the pillow to the end of the bed. Like something had raked it.
They never found him.

## 6. The little bedroom

NARRATOR:
The police said he must have walked out in the night. Mom believed them. I tried to.
I'm thirty-four now. When Mom died, she left me the house. I told myself it was only a house.
My son is nine. He sleeps in the little bedroom at the end of the hall.
This morning, at breakfast, he asked me a question.

SON (9): Mom? Who's the skinny man?
SON: The one who sits at the end of my bed. He doesn't do anything. He just watches.

NARRATOR:
The first time, it only watches.

END (black, silence)
