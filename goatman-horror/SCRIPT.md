# ELEVEN OF US WENT CAMPING. TWELVE CAME BACK.

10-Minute Animated Horror Story — Full Script

A new retelling, in our own words, of the key events of "Anansi's Goatman Story" (an anonymous post on 4chan /x/, 2012, as archived on the Creepypasta Wiki). The characters are renamed, the order is restructured around a cold-open hook, and the language is cleaned up for YouTube.

## Scene 1 — Twelve (0:00–0:40)

MARCUS (Narration — quiet, haunted):

Three packs of hot dogs. Four in each pack.

Twelve.

I know, because I cooked every one of them myself.

Everybody got one.

An hour later, my cousin walked to the stove for seconds, looked into the empty pot, and asked me why I'd taken two.

I hadn't.

There were eleven of us in that trailer.

Twelve hot dogs were gone.

Something had been sitting with us.

Eating with us.

For a whole day.

And not one of us could tell you whose face it was wearing.

## Scene 2 — The Woods (0:40–1:55)

MARCUS (Narration):

I was sixteen the summer my mom sent me down from Chicago to stay with family in Alabama.

My uncle owned a lot of land out there. Miles of pine woods, and two old camping trailers parked deep in the trees.

My cousins couldn't wait to drag the city boy out into the wilderness.

The second we got to the clearing, I noticed the smell.

Sharp. Metallic. Like the air right before lightning hits.

Nobody else seemed to care, so I let it go.

That afternoon, we were swimming in the creek when the bushes moved.

A man stepped out with a shotgun resting in the crook of his arm. A skinny kid about my age was right behind him.

MR. DAWES (Calm, serious):

"You're Earl's people? Good. Listen. Something big's been killing livestock around here. You stick together. And don't go wandering after dark."

MARCUS (Narration):

His son, Wyatt, asked if he could hang out with us.

By sunset, there were eleven of us.

Remember that number.

## Scene 3 — Facing Away (1:55–3:40)

MARCUS (Narration):

Around seven, Wyatt wanted to run home and ask his dad if he could sleep over.

My cousin Dez and a girl named Nia went with him. The rest of us stayed by the fire.

About forty minutes later, the smell came back.

Stronger this time. Like copper. Like the back of your throat after a nosebleed.

We checked the stoves in both trailers. Nothing was on.

Then we heard running.

Wyatt, Dez and Nia burst out of the dark, sprinted straight past the fire, and threw themselves into the trailer.

Dez was crying.

Dez. The toughest person I knew.

WYATT (Terrified, out of breath):

"Lock the door. Nobody goes outside. Nobody!"

MARCUS (Narration):

It took a while to get the story out of them.

A few days earlier, one of Wyatt's pigs had been torn open in his yard.

Tonight, halfway back through the woods, Nia's flashlight had caught a man standing in the trees.

Just standing there.

With his back to them.

A little further on, there was another one on the other side of the trail.

Closer.

Also facing away.

Then a sound started up from both sides of the woods. A low, wet gibbering. Like someone trying to talk with a mouth that wasn't made for it.

Nia swung her light toward it and saw something jerking itself between the trees.

And when they finally saw our campfire, something stepped out onto the trail behind them.

## Scene 4 — The Goatman (3:40–4:30)

MARCUS (Narration):

My little cousin Ray had gone very quiet.

RAY (Whispering):

"It's the Goatman."

MARCUS (Narration):

Everybody told him to shut up.

But he kept going.

People around there told stories about something in those woods. A man's body. A goat's head.

Except you never see it like that.

It wears faces.

It walks into a group of people, sits down among them, and waits for somebody to notice there's one too many.

And then, while Ray was still talking, the smell just stopped.

It didn't fade.

It was there one second and gone the next.

By morning, we'd decided it was some local guys messing with the city kid.

The next day was perfectly normal.

That was the worst part.

## Scene 5 — One Too Many (4:30–5:45)

MARCUS (Narration):

The second night, around one in the morning, we were telling ghost stories by the fire.

The smell came back so thick that one of the girls started gagging.

The air turned cold and wet against my skin.

We crowded into the trailer and nobody wanted to go back out.

So I cooked.

Three packs. Twelve hot dogs.

You already know how that went.

RAY (Annoyed):

"Yo, why'd you take two?"

MARCUS (Inside the scene):

"I didn't. There were twelve. One each for—"

MARCUS (Narration):

I stopped.

And I counted.

Eleven.

That's when Nia started screaming.

NIA (Hysterical):

"Get it out! Get it out of here!"

MARCUS (Narration):

We crashed through the door and spilled out into the clearing.

I counted again.

Eleven.

We went back in with sticks and flashlights.

The trailer was empty.

Later, Nia told us that when she'd started to figure it out, the person beside her had grabbed her knee, hard, leaned close, and whispered something into her ear.

It wasn't words.

She couldn't remember its face.

Neither could any of us.

## Scene 6 — Seven (5:45–7:45)

MARCUS (Narration):

At sunrise, Nia and three others walked out of those woods and went home.

I couldn't. I had my uncle's keys, and somebody had to lock up.

The rest still thought it was a prank, and they wanted to stay.

Wyatt went home to get his rifle.

That left seven of us in the clearing.

At least, that's what we all said.

At five-thirty, somebody said Nia was back.

She was standing by the dead fire pit.

Her back to the trailer.

The smell was back too. Faint.

And the woods had gone completely silent. No birds. No insects. Nothing.

Then her shoulders started to shake.

Like she was laughing.

Without making a sound.

One of the girls started walking out to her, then froze halfway.

MARCUS (Shouting):

"Get back inside. Now!"

MARCUS (Narration):

We pulled every blind except one and watched her through it.

She didn't move for twenty minutes.

Then something slammed against the door.

WYATT (Screaming, outside):

"Let me in! Stop playing!"

MARCUS (Narration):

It was Wyatt, with his rifle.

He said a girl by the fire had turned to watch him cross the clearing.

He never saw her move.

But every time he looked back, she was closer.

He stepped inside, looked around the room, and went pale.

WYATT (Whispering):

"Marcus. Why are there only six of you?"

MARCUS (Narration):

Eleven of us. Four went home. Wyatt left.

Six.

All afternoon, we had been seven.

None of us had done the math.

It had been inside with us again.

And none of us had noticed when it left.

## Scene 7 — Let Me In (7:45–9:10)

MARCUS (Narration):

At sundown, Wyatt's cousin Cole showed up with a lantern and a second rifle.

COLE (Confused):

"Where's your little friend at? She was standing in the middle of the trail. Kept lagging behind me. Just smiling."

MARCUS (Narration):

He'd reached out to touch her shoulder.

His hand closed on empty air.

She was standing a foot to the left.

At eleven, the smell changed.

Burnt hair. Cooked blood.

Something scraped at the door. Half knocking. Half clawing.

Then a voice.

THE VOICE (Wrong, halting, no rhythm):

"Let... me... in. Stop... playing."

MARCUS (Narration):

Wyatt's words.

But wrong.

Every person alive talks with some kind of rhythm. This had none. Like something that had listened to people talk for a very long time but had never been one.

At two in the morning, Cole kicked the door open and fired into the sky.

COLE (Shouting):

"Whatever you are... get gone!"

MARCUS (Narration):

The treeline answered.

Hooting. Gibbering.

Then a scream, like a woman and an animal screaming at the same time.

Something low crawled out of the brush, straight toward the trailer.

Cole fired, and it scrambled back into the dark.

It screamed until almost dawn.

But it never came back to the door.

## Scene 8 — Nine (9:10–10:20)

MARCUS (Narration):

We left at sunrise.

Wyatt stayed behind to lock up. When he caught up with us, he wouldn't look at anyone.

Two days later, he finally told me why.

Around four in the morning, he'd been nodding off in the chair by the door when the bathroom door opened.

Someone walked out, lay down on the floor between us, and closed its eyes.

Wyatt counted.

There were eight of us in that trailer.

There were nine bodies on the floor.

He sat in that chair until morning, pretending to sleep, while every now and then it sat up and shook with that silent laugh.

When he went back to lock up, the bathroom window was wide open.

No screen.

It never had to break in.

And that morning, it walked out of the woods with us.

All the way to Wyatt's house.

Right at the back of the group.

Then it stopped, looked Wyatt dead in the eyes...

and walked back into the trees.

So the next time you're out there with your friends...

count them.

Then count them again.
